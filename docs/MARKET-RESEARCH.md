# Online Quran Teaching — Market Research

**Research date:** 25 November 2026
**Prepared for:** As-Sabuur Quran Academy

---

## Method and honest limitations

This research was compiled from live web search results on 25 November 2026, reading the pricing and course pages surfaced for each provider. Three limitations matter:

1. **Prices are a snapshot.** Online Quran academies change prices and promotions frequently. Every figure below should be re-checked against the provider's own page before it is quoted in a sales conversation or a comparison page.
2. **Published prices are often inconsistent.** Riwaq Al Quran, for example, surfaces both "$32" and "$40" as a starting price on different pages and banners. Where sources disagree, both figures are recorded.
3. **No keyword volume data.** This report does not claim search volumes. Volume figures require Ahrefs, Semrush, or Google Search Console data, which were not available in this environment. The SEO document therefore describes intent and competition qualitatively and marks the exact point where a keyword tool should be applied.

No competitor price, statistic, testimonial or market size figure in this document is estimated or invented. Where something could not be verified, it says so.

## 1. How the market is structured

The international online Quran market has four clearly distinguishable price tiers. They differ less by quality than by where the teacher is based and how the academy positions itself.

| Tier | Typical price (1:1, 30 min, 2×/week) | What it signals |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Budget / South Asia based | $7–$26 per month | High volume, low margin, often Pakistan- or India-based tutors |
| Mainstream international | $35–$45 per month | The modal price; English-language marketing, USD billing |
| Premium | $55–$90 per month | Brand, structured programmes, stronger teacher-vetting language |
| Hour-pool / family | $50–$255 per month | Sold by hours rather than lessons; suited to large families |

UK-based academies cluster tightly at **£25–£50 per month** for two to five 30-minute lessons a week. US-facing academies price in **USD** and usually sit at **$40 per month** for eight 30-minute lessons. That $40 figure is the single most common anchor in the market.

## 3. What the market consistently offers

Across nearly every provider reviewed, five patterns recur:

**Free trials are universal.** Almost every academy offers a free class or a free trial period (1–3 days, or one 30-minute lesson). A trial is table stakes, not a differentiator — but the *form* of the trial is. Most offer "a free class". Very few offer a written assessment or learning plan afterwards.

**30-minute one-to-one lessons are the standard product.** Group classes exist and are cheaper, but the market's default is one teacher, one student, thirty minutes.

**Frequency is the pricing lever, not lesson length.** Most academies ladder their prices by lessons per week (2, 3, 4, 5), then offer 45- and 60-minute variants at a premium.

**No contracts.** Monthly billing with cancellation notice is the norm. Long contracts are a conversion barrier, not a selling point.

**Family and prepayment discounts are widespread.** 7–20% is common, applied to siblings or to 3/6/12-month prepayment.

**Teacher gender choice is a standard request**, particularly for children and for female students. Most academies advertise male and female teachers explicitly.

## 4. Country and market observations

**United States and Canada.** The largest English-language segment. USD pricing is expected. Families compare on trust and scheduling rather than on the lowest price; $40/month is unremarkable to a US household. After-school slots in Eastern and Central time are the highest-demand window.

**United Kingdom.** Prices are set in GBP and cluster at £25–£50. After-school weekday slots (4–7pm) dominate, and Saturday morning is a second peak. UK parents are notably sensitive to safeguarding language — who the teacher is, whether they can sit in, whether the child is ever alone with an unfamiliar adult.

**Australia.** Time-zone distance is a genuine product problem: a Sydney family cannot practically take a lesson at 8pm Gulf time. Academies that schedule in the student's local time and say so explicitly convert better here.

**UAE and Saudi Arabia.** High ability to pay, strong demand for Tajweed and Hifz, and a preference for Arabic-speaking or Ijazah-holding teachers. **No verified published price data was collected for Gulf-specific academies in this research.** Most international academies serve the Gulf at their global USD price rather than a regional one. Treat Gulf pricing as an open question to test, not a settled fact.

**Pakistan, India, Bangladesh, Egypt.** Large supply of teachers and a very low domestic price point ($7–$20). These markets are served by budget academies and are not the target for a premium-positioned brand — but teachers from these countries are the supply base for the entire industry.

## 5. Customer segments, ranked by commercial attractiveness

**1. Parents of children aged 5–14 (highest value).** The largest and most reliable segment. Buying triggers: the child is falling behind in madrasah, the family has moved and lost their local teacher, both parents work and cannot do the school run, or the child is starting from zero. Decision-maker is the mother in most households. The purchase is recurring for years, and siblings multiply the lifetime value. The dominant anxieties are safeguarding, teacher quality, and whether the child will actually enjoy it.

**2. Adults returning to the Quran.** High intent, high emotional stakes, often embarrassed about what they do not know. They want privacy and a teacher who will not make them feel foolish. They convert well when the copy explicitly removes shame ("many adults arrive embarrassed about what they do not know").

**3. Tajweed improvers.** Already read; want precision. They respond to specific technical language (makharij, ghunnah, madd) because it signals real expertise rather than generic marketing.

**4. Hifz students (highest revenue per learner, smallest segment).** Five lessons a week at $100+ per month. Requires daily commitment from the family. Retention is excellent when the plan is realistic and poor when it is oversold. Never promise a completion date.

## 6. What customers appear to value

Based on the offers academies lead with, the recurring objections they answer, and the language used across the category:

- **A real teacher, consistently** — not a rotating pool. "Same teacher every lesson" is a differentiator worth stating.
- **Safeguarding** — parent visibility, teacher gender choice, private lessons.
- **A free trial with no card** — reduces risk to near zero.
- **Transparent monthly pricing** — no registration fee, no hidden materials charge.
- **Scheduling in their time zone** — especially Australia and the US west coast.
- **Evidence of progress** — parents want to know what happened, not just that a lesson occurred.

## 7. Where the category is weak (the opportunity)

Four consistent gaps were visible across competitor sites:

**1. Trials are generic.** Nearly everyone offers a free class; almost nobody promises a written assessment or plan afterwards. A trial that ends with "here is what we recommend and why" is a materially better offer at no extra cost.

**2. Progress reporting is vague.** "Monthly reports" is claimed widely but rarely described. Specificity — what the report contains, who writes it, how often — is a trust differentiator.

**3. Pricing pages hide the price.** Many academies require a form submission to see fees. A clear pricing table is a competitive advantage in a category that obscures it.

**4. Claims outrun evidence.** "Certified", "Ijazah-holding", "thousands of students" are used loosely across the category. For a genuinely established teaching house, the honest version — we taught in person first, here is our method, here is the trial — is more credible than matching the category's inflation.

---

## 8. Strategic implications for As-Sabuur

1. **Do not compete on the lowest price.** The $7–$17 tier is a different business with different economics. It is not winnable at a premium brand position, and it is not the segment that values As-Sabuur's actual advantage.
2. **Do not price above $55 without proof.** Studio Arabiya occupies the premium band with a long-established brand. A newly launched online presence has not yet earned that premium in the eyes of a first-time visitor.
3. **Sit at the upper-middle of the mainstream band.** Above the $40 modal price, below the $55 premium, justified by continuity of teacher, honest assessment, and the in-person heritage. See `PRICING-STRATEGY.md`.
4. **Make the trial the product.** It is the only place where an established teaching house can demonstrate, rather than claim, its advantage.
5. **Lead with parents, but do not neglect adults.** Parents drive volume; adults drive margin and are underserved by copy that treats them as an afterthought.
